# Trailhead 2P Backpacking Tent: User Manual

Model: TH-2P. The Trailhead 2P is a freestanding, double-wall, three-season tent for two people.

## What's in the box

- Tent body
- Rainfly
- Hubbed pole set
- 8 aluminum stakes
- 4 guylines (pre-attached to the fly)
- Stuff sack, pole bag, and stake bag
- Repair kit: pole splint and 2 adhesive fabric patches

## Setting up the tent

1. Choose a flat spot clear of rocks and sticks. Lay out the tent body with the doors facing where you want them.
2. Unfold the pole set. The poles are shock-corded; let each section seat fully so there are no gaps.
3. Insert the four pole tips into the grommets at the corners of the tent body.
4. Clip the tent body's plastic clips onto the poles, starting at the corners and working up to the hub.
5. Drape the rainfly over the tent with the door zippers lined up with the tent doors. Attach the fly's corner buckles to the matching buckles on the tent corners and tighten the straps.
6. Stake out the four corners at a 45° angle, leaning away from the tent. Then stake out the two vestibules.
7. In wind or rain, stake out the four guylines and tension them until the fly is taut and doesn't touch the inner tent.

Setup takes about 5 minutes once you're familiar with the tent. Practice at home before your first trip.

## Taking down and packing

1. Remove the stakes by pulling on the stake loops, not the fabric.
2. Unbuckle and remove the fly, then unclip the tent body from the poles.
3. Fold the poles starting from the middle sections to keep tension even on the shock cord.
4. Fold the tent body and fly loosely and roll them toward the open door to push out air.

## Care and storage

- **Never store the tent wet.** Dry it completely within 24 hours to prevent mildew, which isn't covered by the warranty.
- Clean with a soft sponge, cool water and mild soap. Don't machine-wash, tumble-dry or use detergents.
- Store loosely in a cool, dry place out of direct sunlight. Long-term UV exposure weakens the fly fabric.
- Keep flames, stoves and heaters away from the tent. The fabric isn't fireproof.

## Troubleshooting

**Condensation inside the tent.** Condensation forms when warm, moist air inside meets the cool fly. Open both vestibule vents, keep the doors partly unzipped if weather allows, tension the guylines so the fly doesn't touch the inner tent, and avoid pitching in low, damp spots.

**Leaking at the seams.** The fly seams are factory taped. If a seam leaks, let the fly dry, then apply a silicone seam sealer along the inside of the seam.

**Broken pole section.** Slide the aluminum pole splint from the repair kit over the break and tape both ends. Contact support for a replacement pole section.

**Stuck zipper.** Don't force it. Pull the fabric away from the zipper teeth, ease the slider back, and close it with the door taut. Clean sand from the zipper with water.
